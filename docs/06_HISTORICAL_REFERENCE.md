# SHADOW PROTOCOL: NASSER 07 — Historical Reference (1973)

**Scope:** this document governs Chapter 01 only (6–9 October 1973). Everything after 1973 in
this game is present-day fiction.

**Two rules, non-negotiable:**

1. The 1973 chapter depicts real formations, equipment, uniforms and terrain of the Egyptian
   order of battle as accurately as research allows. Taher never receives modern equipment.
2. **Operation AMUN is invented.** The depot raid, the ledger, the fourteen bodies, and the
   hidden programme are fiction. The game says so in the debrief, in the credits, and in the
   title card — see §6. Historical figures are not placed in fictional scenes.

---

## 1. The crossing, 6 October 1973

- H-Hour was 14:00 local, during Ramadan, chosen partly because the canal's currents, tides
  and moon favoured a daytime crossing.
- The operation opened with a massed artillery and air preparation against the Bar Lev line.
- The sand berm on the Egyptian side was breached with water cannon — high-pressure pumps
  washing away the ramparts — opening lanes for assault boats, bridges and ferries.
- Infantry crossed in rubber assault boats, scaled the Israeli-side ramparts with rope ladders,
  and attacked the strongpoints on foot.
- The Bar Lev line was a chain of fortifications along the canal's east bank, not a continuous
  wall: mutually supporting strongpoints with trenches, bunkers, minefields and wire.

**Depicted in the game:** the barrage, the water-breach, rubber boats, ladders, trench fighting,
bunker clearance, the noise and dust of the crossing. **Compressed:** the timescale — the
chapter's five missions cover several days of the crossing battle in roughly 85 minutes.

## 2. Egyptian equipment (period-correct, as used for the Chapter 01 loadout)

| Category | Items |
|---|---|
| Rifles | AKM (7.62×39) — primary; the AK-47 pattern was widespread, the AKM the most common modernised type |
| Submachine guns | Port Said (9 mm, Egyptian-produced copy of the Swedish Carl Gustaf m/45) |
| Machine guns | RPD (7.62×54R) light machine gun |
| Anti-tank | RPG-7, B-10/B-11 recoilless rifles, AT-3 Sagger (brigade-level) |
| Sniper | SVD (7.62×54R) |
| Sidearms | Tokarev TT-33 |
| Grenades | RGD-5, F1 |
| Uniforms | Sand/khaki field uniform, steel helmet (Egyptian pattern), canvas webbing, leather boots |
| Support | BTR-50/BTR-60 APCs, T-55/T-62 tanks, Mi-8 helicopters, pontoon bridges, rubber assault boats |

**Explicitly excluded from 1973:** night vision optics, red-dot or holographic sights,
rail systems, polymer furniture, modern body armour, GPS, digital radios, drones, suppressors
as standard issue.

---

## 3. Terrain and atmosphere

- West bank: staging areas, earthworks, vehicle parks, field kitchens, telephone wire.
- The canal: 160–180 m wide, steep revetted banks, strong current.
- East bank: sand ramparts, then the strongpoint belt — trenches, bunkers, minefields, wire.
- The Qantara road: a supply road running east, with depots behind the line.
- Weather in early October: hot, dry, dust haze; nights cold enough for a greatcoat.

## 4. Kit authenticity checklist (for art review)

Before any 1973 asset is approved:

- [ ] No post-1973 materials (no nylon tactical gear, no velcro, no MOLLE)
- [ ] Helmet profile matches Egyptian pattern, not M1 or SSh-40
- [ ] Weapon furniture is wood or stamped steel, never polymer
- [ ] Insignia and rank markings consistent with the Egyptian Army of 1973
- [ ] Arabic signage uses period typography and spelling
- [ ] No English-language packaging or signage in Egyptian rear areas
- [ ] Vehicle markings plausible for the formation depicted

---

## 5. Language

- Egyptian colloquial Arabic for soldiers; formal Arabic for officers addressing formations and
  for radio procedure.
- Period radio procedure: clipped, formulaic, repeated back.
- Transliterated ranks follow Egyptian usage (عقيد / رائد / رقيب), not US equivalents.

## 7. Sources to consult during art production

- Egyptian official histories of the October War (armed forces publications)
- Unit-level accounts and memoirs of canal-crossing infantry
- Photographic archives of the Egyptian Army, 1973 (uniforms, helmets, webbing, vehicles)
- Period photographs of the canal's west and east banks, Qantara road, and Sinai depots

Cross-check every visual decision against at least two independent sources. Where sources
disagree, choose the more conservative depiction and note the uncertainty in the art ticket.

---

## 8. What this document is not

It is not a claim of scholarly authority. It is the production constraint sheet that keeps a
fictional game from misrepresenting a real war. Any correction from a historian should
override anything written here.
